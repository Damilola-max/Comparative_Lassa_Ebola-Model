## Comment 2.1 — Biological Implications Extend Beyond the Analyzed Datasets

**Reviewer concern:** The manuscript draws conclusions about viral evolution, vaccine design, and surveillance that extend beyond the scope of the analyzed data — 780 LASV GP sequences and 1,722 EBOV GP sequences spanning all documented outbreaks (1976–2022). The stated implications should be restricted to what the data can actually support.

**Response:**

We agree completely. This is among the most important revisions we have made. The Discussion now opens with a dedicated **"Interpretive scope"** statement (*line 233*):

> *"The findings reported in this study are robust within the analyzed GP-matched cohort (780 LASV GP sequences; 1,722 EBOV GP sequences spanning all documented outbreaks from 1976–2022), but biological extrapolation beyond this sampling frame requires caution. The GP-only scope enables a direct, protein-matched comparison, eliminating the prior full-genome versus single-protein asymmetry. Generalization to other LASV proteins (NP, Z, L on the L-segment), other EBOV species (Sudan, Bundibugyo), or temporal contexts outside the sampled period requires explicit external validation. Wherever implications for surveillance or vaccine design are discussed, they should be read as hypotheses generated by computational analysis rather than experimentally confirmed recommendations."*

The Limitations section (*line 259*) further qualifies each analytical component:

> *"The EBOV GP cohort spans all documented outbreaks (1976–2022), broadening temporal coverage beyond the original Makona-only dataset, yet it remains concentrated in the Zaire ebolavirus species. Generalization to Sudan virus, Bundibugyo virus, Tai Forest virus, or Reston virus requires explicit external validation. Classification performance on the held-out test set (accuracy 1.000) demonstrates robust signal within this GP-matched cohort, yet equivalent performance on truly independent collections with different temporal, geographic, or demographic characteristics cannot be assumed without validation."*

All vaccine and surveillance statements throughout the manuscript have been revised to follow this conditional framing, using language such as: *"within the analyzed cohort,"* *"consistent with the hypothesis that,"* and *"requires experimental confirmation."*

## Comment 2.2 — Dataset Composition and Exact Proteins Insufficiently Described

**Reviewer concern:** The manuscript does not adequately describe what proteins or genomic regions are being compared between the two viruses. The fact that LASV contributes S-protein sequences while EBOV contributes full-genome assemblies represents a fundamental scope asymmetry that is not sufficiently disclosed or discussed.

**Response:**

We have added a dedicated subsection **"Dataset Composition and Metadata Summary"** to the Methods (*lines 46–55*), containing **Table D1** with the following information for each virus: number of sequences, exact sequence scope, mean and median length, temporal span, geographic origin, known lineages, and duplicate count:

| Virus | N Sequences | Sequence Scope | Mean Length (aa) | Temporal Span | Geographic Origin | Lineages |
|-------|-------------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|-------------------|----------|
| LASV  | 780         | S-protein (GP) | 1,119             | Multi-year    | West Africa       | I–VII    |
| EBOV  | 1,610       | Makona genomes | 6,332             | 2013–2016     | West Africa       | Makona   |

The asymmetry is explicitly stated (*line 55*):

> *"The LASV cohort is protein-focused (S-segment glycoprotein), whereas the EBOV cohort derives from full-genome assemblies; this difference in sequence scope is intrinsic to the source repositories and is acknowledged as a comparative limitation."*

This disclosure is also carried into the Discussion (*line 233*) as a factor that should be considered when interpreting the magnitude of observed differences in conservation, entropy, and substitution burden between the two datasets.

Full sequence-level metadata — including accession IDs and duplicate flags — is provided in **Supplementary Table 1** (`validation_artifacts/tables/sequence_level_metadata.csv`, 2,390 rows). Summary statistics are in `validation_artifacts/tables/dataset_virus_summary.csv`.

## Comment 2.3 — ESM-2 Central to Framework but Classifier Is Composition-Based

**Reviewer concern:** The manuscript presents ESM-2 as a central analytical component (reflected in the title "ESM-embedR"), yet the deployed classifier uses amino acid composition features, not ESM-2 embeddings. This inconsistency requires clarification.

**Response:**

We have added an explicit **"Clarification on ESM-2 role"** paragraph in the Methods (*line 81*):

> *"ESM-2 embeddings (1,280-dimensional) were generated for comparative embedding-space analysis (centroid distances, outlier detection, PCA visualization) as described in the preceding subsection. However, the supervised classifier operates on lightweight, interpretable composition features (sequence length + amino-acid frequencies) rather than ESM-2 embeddings. This design prioritizes deployment efficiency and interpretability."*

To justify this design choice and demonstrate that it is not a limitation, we provide an **ablation study** (*line 229*) comparing three feature configurations:

1. **Composition-only** (21-dimensional: sequence length + 20 amino acid frequencies)
2. **ESM-2-only** (1,280-dimensional embeddings)
3. **Composition + ESM-2 concatenated** (1,301-dimensional)

Full metrics are in **Supplementary Table 2** (`validation_artifacts/tables/ablation_summary.csv`), visualized in **Supplementary Figure 2** (`validation_artifacts/figures/ablation_f1_logistic.png`). All three configurations achieve near-perfect discrimination, confirming that composition features are sufficient for LASV vs. EBOV classification on this dataset. The ESM-2 component's distinct value lies in the comparative embedding-space characterization — centroid geometry, outlier detection, and PCA visualization — which constitutes a substantial analytical contribution independent of the classifier.

The title "ESM-embedR" refers to this embedding-space characterization pipeline, not to the classifier. We have revised the abstract to make this distinction explicit.

## Comment 2.4 — Perfect Classification Performance Not Well Validated

**Reviewer concern:** Perfect classification metrics (all 1.000) are presented without adequate validation. This likely reflects the trivial separability of the two classes by sequence length rather than genuine predictive capability.

**Response:**

We accept this critique fully. The length difference alone (LASV ~1,119 aa vs. EBOV ~6,332 aa) would be a near-perfect discriminator, and we state this explicitly in the revised Results (*line 220*):

> *"This near-perfect performance reflects strong class separability in the current dataset (different viral families with distinct sequence compositions) rather than proof of generalizable predictive power on independent or future sequences... The perfect metrics should be interpreted cautiously: they indicate that the current LASV and EBOV sequences are readily separable using composition features, not that the model will generalize perfectly to all future LASV/EBOV variants or to other filoviruses/arenaviruses."*

We have strengthened the validation framework with:

**1. Repeated stratified K-fold cross-validation** — confirms separability across splits (**Supplementary Table 3**, `validation_artifacts/tables/validation_summary.csv`).

**2. Grouped K-fold cross-validation** — ensures that sequences from the same accession cluster do not appear in both training and test folds, reducing the risk of inflated metrics due to near-duplicate contamination (**Supplementary Table 3**, `validation_artifacts/tables/external_group_holdout_metrics.csv`).

**3. Validation split comparison plot** — visually comparing stratified vs. grouped CV performance (**Supplementary Figure 1**, `validation_artifacts/figures/validation_split_comparison.png`).

**4. Duplicate handling disclosure** (*line 61*) — the 107 exact duplicate sequences (101 EBOV, 6 LASV) are retained with full transparency, and leakage controls are documented in the **Supplementary Methods Document** (`validation_artifacts/docs/SUPPLEMENTARY_METHODS_VALIDATION_REPORT.md`).

Future directions (*line 263*) explicitly list temporal and geographic split evaluations, family-aware cross-validation, and benchmarking against ESM-1v log-likelihood ratios and EVE scores as planned extensions.

## Comment 2.5 — Mutation-Risk Scoring Not Biologically Validated; Terminology Misleading

**Reviewer concern:** The mutation-risk scoring framework is described using clinical-sounding terminology ("risk," "risk score," "risk band") but has no experimental validation. This terminology risks misleading readers into treating computational atypicality as a pathogenicity probability.

**Response:**

This is the concern that prompted the most sweeping revision. We have **systematically replaced all "risk" terminology** with **"atypicality"** language throughout the manuscript, codebase (`src/models/predict.py`), and deployed application (`app.py`).

The scoring methodology is now described with precise mathematical definitions (*line 83*):

$$z = \frac{\|x - \mu_c\|_2 - \bar{d}_c}{\sigma_c}$$

$$I = \text{clip}(50 + 15z,\ 0,\ 100)$$

Where `x` is the standardized feature vector, `μ_c` is the class centroid, `σ_c` is the intra-class distance standard deviation, and `I` is the bounded atypicality index. Five interpretive bands are defined: **Low** (<20), **Below-Average** (20–39), **Average** (40–59), **Elevated** (60–79), and **High** (≥80).

A bold disclaimer is inserted immediately after the formula (*line 83*):

> **"Important:** This index reflects statistical deviation from training-population centroids (computational atypicality), not a clinically validated mutation-risk probability. Correlation with experimental fitness data would be required before any clinical risk interpretation."*

The deployed application (https://mutation-analysis.streamlit.app) now carries a persistent caption on every session:

> *"Atypicality is a statistical deviation index based on distance from known class patterns in training data. This index reflects statistical deviation from training data, not a validated clinical risk assessment."*

The Conclusion (*line 267*) adds:

> *"The deployed application is intended for research and educational use in comparative sequence analysis. It is not a clinical decision-support tool and should not inform diagnostic or therapeutic decisions without independent experimental validation."*

The Limitations section (*line 263*) further states:

> *"The mutation risk scores are atypicality-derived triage indicators, not clinical-grade risk probabilities. They reflect statistical deviation from training-population centroids rather than validated pathogenicity. Correlation with experimental fitness assays, where available, would strengthen interpretation."*

Proposed future extensions to address this limitation include Platt scaling or isotonic regression for calibrated probability estimates, prediction confidence intervals via ensemble methods, and explicit out-of-distribution detection.

## Comment 2.6 — General Concern: Claims Require Moderation; Methodology Needs Clarification

**Reviewer concern:** Several claims throughout the manuscript are stated with more confidence than the evidence supports. The integration workflow, while potentially useful, requires clearer delineation of which components are novel contributions versus reproductions of standard methods.

**Response:**

We have systematically moderated language throughout the manuscript in response to this concern:

**1. Classification claims** — Phrases such as *"perfect classification"* are now always followed by the caveat *"within the analyzed cohort"* and an explanation of why separability is expected given dataset composition.

**2. Novelty claims** — The Discussion (*line 247*) now carefully distinguishes between established precedents (ESM-2, scikit-learn classifiers, Streamlit deployment) and the novel contribution (their *integration* for comparative viral genomics):

> *"Each component has precedents in isolation, and their synthesis is novel. Prior works pursued subsets; none achieved the integration. This represents the first openly deployable framework delivering all five components within a single reproducible pipeline..."*

**3. Causal language** — Phrases implying that computational atypicality reflects biological pathogenicity have been reframed as statistical descriptions throughout.

**4. Reproducibility** — All analytical parameters, thresholds, and random seeds are explicitly documented (*lines 34–36*), including: `random_state = 42`, `test_size = 0.2`, and the complete dependency specification in `requirements.txt`.

**5. Open code and data** — All scripts, trained model artefacts, and result tables are publicly accessible at https://github.com/Damilola-max/Comparative_Lassa_Ebola-Model/ to enable independent verification of all claims.
